Social media marketing is one of the lowest-cost channels for a new small business. Posting consistently on platforms where the target customer already spends time (Instagram for visual/lifestyle products, LinkedIn for B2B services, WhatsApp/Facebook groups for local audiences) builds awareness without large ad spend.

A simple marketing strategy for a new business should include: defining a clear target customer profile, choosing 1-2 primary channels instead of spreading effort across every platform, creating a consistent posting schedule, and tracking which posts/campaigns actually drive inquiries or sales.

Word-of-mouth and referral marketing remain highly effective for local and service-based businesses. Offering a small referral incentive (discount, free add-on) to existing customers who bring in new customers is a low-cost way to grow steadily.

Paid advertising (social media ads, search ads) can accelerate growth once a business has validated its offering with organic marketing, but new businesses should start with small test budgets and measure cost-per-customer-acquired before scaling ad spend significantly.

Content marketing — such as short videos, how-to posts, or behind-the-scenes content — helps build trust with potential customers before they buy, especially for higher-priced products/services where buyers want to understand quality before purchasing.

Branding basics for a new business include a consistent name, logo, color scheme, and tone of voice across all customer touchpoints (packaging, social media, website). Consistent branding helps a small business look more trustworthy and professional to first-time customers.

Local marketing tactics such as partnering with nearby complementary businesses, participating in local events/markets, and getting listed on local search platforms (like Google Business Profile) are especially effective for brick-and-mortar or delivery-based businesses.
