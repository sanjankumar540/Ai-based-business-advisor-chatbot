Low-investment business ideas for students and first-time entrepreneurs include tutoring services, freelance content writing, social media management for small local shops, print-on-demand merchandise, and reselling curated products online. These require limited upfront capital and can be tested part-time before committing full resources.

Home-based food businesses such as tiffin/meal delivery services, baked goods, and snack packaging are popular first businesses because kitchen space is often already available and demand is recurring (daily or weekly orders) rather than one-time.

Clothing and fashion businesses can start small with a curated online catalog (Instagram/WhatsApp storefront) before investing in physical retail space. Starting with a narrow niche (e.g., ethnic wear, sustainable fashion, plus-size clothing) reduces competition compared to a general clothing store.

Service-based businesses such as home cleaning, tutoring, event planning, and repair services typically require lower upfront investment than product-based businesses because there is no inventory to purchase, though they depend heavily on the founder's or team's time and skill.

Technology-enabled small businesses such as building simple websites for local shops, basic app development, or digital marketing consulting can be started with a laptop and internet connection, making them attractive low-capital options for technically skilled founders.

E-commerce and dropshipping businesses allow founders to sell products without holding inventory, by forwarding orders to a supplier who ships directly to the customer. This lowers upfront investment risk but usually results in lower profit margins per unit compared to holding your own stock.

Franchise businesses (food, retail, or service franchises) provide an established brand, operating playbook, and marketing support in exchange for a franchise fee and ongoing royalty payments. They typically require more upfront capital than an independent business but carry lower brand-building risk.
